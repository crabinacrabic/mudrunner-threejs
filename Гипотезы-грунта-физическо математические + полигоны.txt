# Research task: stylized off-road terrain and mud physics for Three.js

## Goal

Develop and validate a plausible, computationally inexpensive mud, soil, water and tire-interaction model for a cozy stylized off-road exploration game inspired by Over the Hill.

Target stack: Three.js, TypeScript or JavaScript, WebGL-compatible rendering. WebGPU compute may be evaluated as an optional enhancement.

Do not copy proprietary game assets. Use original or properly licensed GLB/GLTF assets.

## Phase 1 — Research and mathematical review

Before modifying the code, critically assess these proposed models:

1. Herschel–Bulkley rheology for the transition from yield-stress soil to mobile mud slurry.
2. Bekker pressure–sinkage modelling for wheel-induced soil deformation.
3. Janosi–Hanamoto shear stress modelling for traction under wheel slip.
4. A custom tire-clogging state variable coupled to soil moisture, solids concentration and wheel rotation.
5. A shallow-layer water and suspended-sediment model for puddles and muddy shorelines.

Identify which parts are established physical approximations and which are gameplay heuristics. Check the equations, units, boundary conditions and assumptions. Do not claim this model is scientifically validated.

Useful references:

- Mudflow rheology review: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012825225001874
- Wheel–soil interaction model: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-19829-7
- OpenAI mathematical research collection: https://github.com/openai/math

The OpenAI math repository is a collection of mathematical manuscripts and proof artifacts, not a ready-made game-physics library. Investigate relevant results only if they offer a concrete numerical method or useful mathematical tool.

## Phase 2 — State representation

For each terrain cell, maintain:

- moisture saturation S in [0, 1];
- mobile slurry solids mass fraction phi_s in [0, 1];
- compaction C in [0, 1];
- persistent rut depth r in meters;
- surface water depth h_w in meters;
- mobile sediment mass per unit area m_s;
- base terrain elevation z0.

For each tire, maintain a normalized clogging state C_t in [0, 1].

Use a fixed simulation timestep. Start with a coarse grid, around 64×64 or 128×128 cells for an active terrain region, and update cells near the wheels and water edges at higher frequency if necessary.

## Phase 3 — Hypothesis equations

Define the wetness-driven transition:

lambda = smoothstep(S0, S1, S)

Provisional yield-stress hypothesis:

tau_y = (1 - lambda) * tau_d * (1 + k_compaction * C)
       + lambda * tau_mud
       * max(0, (phi_s - phi_c) / (1 - phi_c))^p

Use a Herschel–Bulkley relation for the mobile slurry:

|tau| = tau_y + K * |shearRate|^n

Use a stable regularization near zero shear rate. Prevent fluid flow below the yield threshold. For diluted mixtures, test the limit where yield stress approaches zero and viscosity approaches a water-like value.

Use the Bekker pressure–sinkage relationship:

p = (kc / tireWidth + kPhi) * sinkage^nB

Treat its soil parameters as moisture- and compaction-dependent. Verify dimensions and make sure wetter soil produces deeper sinkage for equivalent loading when configured as softer soil.

Use the Janosi–Hanamoto relationship:

tau_soil = (cohesion + normalStress * tan(frictionAngle))
           * (1 - exp(-shearDisplacement / shearModulus))

Check sign conventions and slip behaviour before integrating wheel forces.

For tire clogging, evaluate:

dC_t/dt =
  k_load * adhesion(S) * phi_s * abs(slip) * (1 - C_t)
  - (k_eject * (wheelAngularSpeed / referenceSpeed)^2
     + k_clean * abs(vehicleSpeed)) * C_t

Use a smooth adhesion curve that peaks at intermediate moisture, but treat this as a testable gameplay hypothesis rather than established physics.

## Phase 4 — Water and sediment

Implement water depth as a separate field from soil moisture. A wet ground cell does not automatically contain a visible puddle.

Use a conservative grid-based water-flux approximation for surface water. Gate yield-stress mud motion by the comparison between gravitational shear stress and yield stress.

Track water volume and sediment mass. Transport the suspended fraction with water flow and deposit sediment when the flow slows. Include shoreline mixing, shallow turbid water, puddles, drainage and terrain slope.

Do not implement a full 3D Navier–Stokes solver unless testing shows a clear benefit over a shallow-water approximation.

## Phase 5 — Three.js visualization

Use a data texture or equivalent field representation to communicate wetness, rut depth, puddle depth and compaction to the terrain shader.

Render:

- dry dirt with high roughness;
- moist ground with progressively darker colour and reduced roughness;
- sticky mud with visible deformation and wheel ruts;
- puddles with a separate water surface;
- sediment plumes and splashes near the shoreline;
- tire dirt accumulation that increases with C_t.

Prefer stylized, warm colours, soft fog, golden grass, pastel skies, turquoise water and simplified vegetation over photorealism.

Keep texture resolution and draw calls practical for a browser game. Use instancing for repeated rocks and trees.

## Phase 6 — Required validation

Build small deterministic tests before integrating into the main game:

1. Dry compacted soil: negligible fluid movement and shallow rutting.
2. Moist soil: gradual reduction in strength, increased sinkage and visible tire marks.
3. Sticky mud: strong resistance, increasing clogging during high slip and reduced traction.
4. Saturated, diluted mud: lower yield stress and greater flow.
5. Shoreline: water transports sediment without creating or destroying mass.
6. Wheel clearing: tire clogging decreases under the selected cleaning/ejection conditions.
7. Numerical stability: no negative water depth, no NaNs, bounded state variables and no unbounded rut growth.

Add debug views for S, C, rut depth, water depth, slurry concentration and tire clogging.

Measure water-volume and sediment-mass drift in closed-system tests. Report errors and performance.

## Deliverable

First return a review of equations, assumptions, unknown parameters and numerical risks. Do not implement the complete model before this review.

Then propose an incremental implementation plan with separate modules, unit tests and clear visual acceptance criteria. Preserve existing project architecture and avoid unrelated rewrites.

Это метод моделирования материалов, который сочетает частицы с вычислительной сеткой. Он особенно интересен для задач, где материал деформируется, течёт или ведёт себя как упругое тело.
Официальный пример Three.js — Fluid Particles уже использует MLS-MPM в вычислительных шейдерах.

Система Метод
Жидкая вода и лужи  Двумерная модель мелкой воды
Вязкая грязь и комки  MLS-MPM с подходящей моделью материала
Деформация дороги и колеи Сетка грунта + модель давления колеса
Скольжение и забивание протектора Модель сдвига грунта и отдельное состояние загрязнения шин
Мягкие объекты  XPBD или метод конечных элементов
Рендеринг грязи WebGPU/Three.js, карты влажности и глубины колеи
Это гибридная система, а не единый решатель для всей сцены. Она позволит считать сложную физику преимущественно рядом с автомобилем, а удалённый ландшафт обновлять намного дешевле.
Мой вывод: Navier–Stokes и OpenAI Math стоит использовать как исследовательскую основу, но непосредственно для грязи я бы начал с MLS-MPM. Он уже реализован в экосистеме Three.js и ближе к задаче смешения деформируемого грунта и жидкости. Следующий шаг — поручить Claude Code исследовать MLS-MPM, Herschel–Bulkley и модели взаимодействия шин с грунтом, а затем сравнить их по стабильности, производительности и визуальному результату на твоей RX 6600.
